# How to Buy Tiles Online in Australia: A Practical Buying Guide

Buying tiles online is straightforward once you know what to look for. This guide walks you through format, finish, lead times, and how to avoid the mistakes that turn a renovation into a second renovation.

---

## Why Buy Tiles Online

The Saturday showroom run is a known misery. You drive out to a trade precinct, a salesperson steers you toward whatever needs moving, and you leave with a brochure and a headache.

Buying direct online gives you time to think. You can compare formats side by side, read technical specs without someone hovering, and order samples before you commit to a pallet. For most residential projects, that is a better process.

The catch is that you are making colour and texture decisions from a screen. Tile photography varies wildly between manufacturers, and a honed marble-look porcelain photographed in a European studio can look completely different under Australian afternoon light in your bathroom. Samples fix this. More on that below.

## Step 1 — Get the Format Right

Format means the physical dimensions of the tile. It is the single decision that changes a room most.

**Large format (600×1200mm and above)**
Fewer grout lines, so a space reads as calmer and larger. Works well in open-plan living areas, full-height feature walls, and bathrooms where you want a seamless feel. Requires a very flat substrate and an experienced tiler, because lippage (one tile edge sitting proud of the next) is more visible at large format.

**Medium format (600×600mm, 600×900mm)**
The workhorse of residential projects. Easier to lay on floors that have minor variation. Good for bathrooms, laundries, and outdoor areas where full 600×1200 sheets add unnecessary weight and cost to the job.

**Smaller formats (mosaic through to 300×600mm)**
Feature walls, shower niches, and splashbacks. Mosaics add texture and visual interest but take longer to lay, so tiling labour costs go up. Budget for it.

**A note on proportion**
Match format to room size. A 100×600mm timber-look tile in a small bathroom reads as floorboards. A 600×1200 marble-look slab in a narrow ensuite reads as a hotel corridor. Neither is wrong, but walk through the logic before you order.

## Step 2 — Choose the Right Finish

Finish affects both how a tile looks and how it performs over time.

**Polished**
High gloss. Reflects light well, makes marble-look and Calacatta-style porcelain look closer to the real stone. Shows water marks, footprints, and cleaning residue. Fine in low-traffic areas and walls. A poor choice for busy family floors.

**Honed**
Flat, low-sheen surface. More forgiving of marks. Closer to the natural stone feel of travertine or limestone. Works on floors and walls. A practical choice for bathrooms that see daily use.

**Textured / anti-slip**
Required for outdoor pavers and pool surrounds under Australian standards. Also sensible for internal wet areas if you have children or older household members. Look for a slip rating (P-rating or R-rating) on the product spec sheet and confirm it suits your application with your tiler.

**Matt**
Similar to honed but typically a manufactured surface rather than a cut stone reference. Concrete-look tiles are usually matt. Hides dirt well. Consistent across the range.

**Zellige and handmade-look**
Intentional variation in glaze, surface, and sizing. Each tile is slightly different. This is the point. Expect movement in shade and texture across a wall. If you want perfect uniformity, Zellige is the wrong product. If you want character, it is the right one.

## Step 3 — Understand Material Categories

All tiles in this guide refer to porcelain and stone. Nothing else.

**Porcelain**
Fired at high temperature, dense, low porosity. The most practical material for Australian conditions. Most marble-look, travertine-look, concrete-look, timber-look, and terrazzo tiles on the market are porcelain. They are not trying to deceive you. They are engineered to perform in ways natural stone cannot, particularly in wet areas and outdoors.

**Natural stone**
Marble, travertine, and limestone have natural variation that porcelain cannot fully replicate. They also require sealing, can be acid-sensitive, and need more care over time. If you want the real thing and are willing to maintain it, natural stone is a considered choice. If you want the look without the upkeep, porcelain is the sensible alternative.

**Zellige**
Handmade Moroccan-origin glazed clay tile. Not porcelain. Not stone. A distinct category. High variation, rich depth of colour, and tactile surface. Increasingly used on splashbacks and feature walls in Australian homes.

## Step 4 — Order Samples Before You Order Anything Else

This is non-negotiable on any project over a few square metres.

Tile photography online is shot under controlled studio light by people whose job is to make tiles look beautiful. Your kitchen, bathroom, or living room is lit differently, has different wall colours, and has different proportions. A tile you love on screen can look flat, cold, or too busy in context.

The right process:

1. Shortlist three to five tiles you are seriously considering.
2. Order samples and receive them at the actual property.
3. Hold them against your cabinetry, wall colour, and grout sample.
4. Look at them at different times of day, especially in the light conditions that matter most (morning in a bathroom, afternoon in a living area).
5. Make your decision from the physical tile, not the screen.
