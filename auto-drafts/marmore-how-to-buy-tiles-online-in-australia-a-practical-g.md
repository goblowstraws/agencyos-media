# How to Buy Tiles Online in Australia: A Practical Guide

Buying tiles online saves a Saturday. It also removes the one thing that makes showroom visits useful — holding a sample under your own light, against your own floor, next to your own paint swatch. This guide explains how to do it properly, from working out how much tile you need through to receiving a full order at your door.

---

## Work Out Your Area First

Measure every surface you intend to tile. Length multiplied by width gives you square metres. Do each wall or floor separately, then add them together.

Add a waste allowance on top:

- **Straight lay, simple room:** 10% extra
- **Diagonal or herringbone lay:** 15% extra
- **Oddly shaped room or lots of cuts around obstacles:** 15–20% extra

Tiles come in boxes, and boxes cover a fixed area. You will almost always need to round up to the next full box. Order short and you risk a second freight charge and a lead-time gap that stalls the whole job.

---

## Understand How Tiles Are Priced

Tiles are sold per box and per square metre. The per-m² figure is the useful one for budgeting; multiply it by your total area (including waste allowance) to get a baseline material cost.

Freight is always a separate line at checkout, calculated by postcode and order size. It is never included in the tile price. Factor it in before you commit to a budget figure.

## Choose the Right Format for the Job

Format means the tile dimensions. It affects how the room reads visually and how the installer prices the job.

**Large format (600×1200 and above)**
Fewer grout lines, so walls and floors read as a cleaner, more continuous surface. Better suited to open-plan spaces and larger bathrooms. Requires a very flat substrate — your tiler will tell you if prep work is needed. Heavier boxes mean higher freight on some orders.

**Mid format (300×600, 600×600)**
The most common format range. Works in most residential rooms and suits most installation styles. Easier to cut around obstacles.

**Small format and mosaics**
More labour-intensive to lay, but useful for feature walls, niches, and anywhere a large tile would look out of proportion.

**Plank and linear formats (timber-look tiles, Zellige)**
Follow the length of the room rather than a square grid. Layout planning matters more here — talk to your tiler before ordering.

---

## Choose the Right Finish

**Polished and honed**
Reflective surfaces make a room feel larger and brighter. Polished tiles show smudges and water marks more readily than matte. Honed sits between the two — a low-sheen, smooth surface that is easier to maintain than polished while still reading as refined.

**Matt**
Lower slip risk underfoot, so a practical default for bathroom floors and outdoor areas. Hides everyday grime better than polished.

**Textured and structured**
Designed for slip resistance. The R-rating system (R9 through R13) tells you how much grip a tile offers. For outdoor areas and wet zones, check the R-rating before you order. A tiler or builder can advise what is required by Australian standards for your specific application.

## Tile Categories Worth Knowing

Marmore stocks ranges across several looks. Here is a plain-English description of what each one means:

**Marble Look porcelain**
Porcelain tiles that replicate the veining and movement of natural marble — Calacatta, Statuario, Nero, and similar. Far more consistent in patterning than natural stone, easier to maintain, and significantly more durable in wet areas. No sealing required.

**Travertine Look**
Replicates the warm, earthy texture of travertine stone. The porcelain version skips the pit-filling and sealing that real travertine demands.

**Terrazzo**
A speckle-and-chip pattern originally from Italian floors. Works as a standalone statement or as a complement to plain tiles in the same room.

**Concrete Look**
Industrial grey tones, low visual noise. Suits modern and Scandi-influenced interiors. Available across multiple formats and usually in both matt and polished finishes.

**Timber Look**
Porcelain planks that replicate timber grain. None of the moisture sensitivity of real timber, so usable in bathrooms and laundries where real timber cannot go.

**Zellige**
Handmade-look glazed tiles with natural variation in colour and surface. Each tile reads slightly differently, which is the point. Suits feature walls, splashbacks, and smaller tiled areas where variation reads as texture rather than inconsistency.

**Outdoor and pavers**
Thicker gauge, higher R-rating, built for external use. Check that whatever you choose is rated for your specific application — pool surrounds, driveways, and balconies each have different requirements.

## Order Samples Before You Commit

This is the single most useful thing you can do before placing a full tile order.

Colours shift under different light. A tile that reads warm white on a screen can read cool grey under your LED downlights. A dark charcoal concrete-look tile can look completely different in a south-facing bathroom versus a north-facing one.

Marmore's sample pack is five tiles, ten centimetres square, for fifteen dollars flat — shipped anywhere in Australia. The fifteen dollars is credited back against your first full order. Take the samples home and look at them in situ: against your walls, your cabinetry, your hardware. Come back when you are sure.

That is the whole point of the funnel. People who are serious about their renovation pay fifteen dollars to be certain. People who just want to browse stay on the website.

## Understand Lead Times Before You Book Your Tiler

This is where a lot of online tile purchases go wrong. People book their tiler, then order tiles.

Marmore operates on a manual fulfilment model. Each order is placed individually with the importer after purchase. Lead times run in bands:

- **In-stock product:** typically 5–10 business days from order to delivery, depending on your location
- **Indent or non-stocked product:** longer lead times apply — the site will indicate this where known

The practical rule: order tiles before you book your tiler, not after. Give yourself buffer on top of the estimated lead time. Renovations almost always find ways to use extra time.
